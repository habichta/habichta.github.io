One model per time series? Chronos-2 thinks that is so last paradigm. A short and only mildly informed look at foundation models for forecasting.

For decades, time series forecasting was run like a daycare with a very strict staffing ratio: every single series got its own model, lovingly fitted, tuned and burped by a sleep-deprived data scientist.

Then came pretrained "foundation models", which according to Ansari et al. triggered nothing less than a paradigm shift1: instead of training a model for each time series (local models) or for each dataset (task-specific models), you train one big model and let it do the forecasting for everyone. The latest of these is Chronos-2, named after the Greek personification of time2, who presumably got tired of waiting for our hyperparameter searches to finish.

The title promises a journey "from univariate to universal", the most ambitious glow-up since my Excel trendline started calling itself AI. Think of it as a fortune teller who has read so many tea leaves that your particular cup no longer surprises it. I have only made it through page one so far, so please treat this post as a forecast with very wide confidence intervals3.

[1] Ansari et al. (2025): Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting. A rare case where "paradigm shift" was not written to get a grant approved.
[2] Not to be confused with Cronus, the Titan who ate his own children. Easy mix-up, very different evaluation metrics.
[3] See the orange cone in the header image. The wider the cone, the harder it is to be wrong.
